Yes. Kuala Lumpur is stronger than I gave it credit for, provided the map is really Klang Valley, not just the Kuala Lumpur municipal boundary. I would now put it around the Stuttgart / Zürich level of interest, and probably above Montréal for INTERCITY. Why it works The underlying geography is useful without being tyrannical. Kuala Lumpur has broad river plains but also steep-sided hills to the west, north-east and south, with the Klang valley opening south-westwards. That creates friction and route preference, but does not turn the whole map into Genoa-style corridors. More importantly, Klang Valley is a continuous but polycentric urban field. Kuala Lumpur, Petaling Jaya, Subang Jaya, Shah Alam, Puchong, Kajang and the Putrajaya/Cyberjaya axis create lots of destinations that can be connected in different combinations. It is not Greater Bay Area, where the metropolitan components remain much more obviously separate cities. The real network is unusually layered. Official network maps contain KTM Komuter, several LRT lines, two MRT lines, the monorail, airport rail and Sunway BRT. The Putrajaya MRT alone is 57.7 kilometres long and has ten interchange or connecting stations, so this is already a network in which ostensibly different service classes repeatedly meet rather than living in isolation. But I would not turn all of those technologies into separate INTERCITY modes. That would be the trap. I think the useful mode structure is only three layers Rapid transit Combine MRT and LRT conceptually. They differ technically in reality, but for the game they largely perform the same job: high-frequency urban and metropolitan rail with relatively close stations. Give the player substantial alignment freedom. Regional rail Use KTM Komuter as the distinct heavier mode. Fewer stops, longer reach, higher capacity and faster outer-suburban travel. It should nevertheless penetrate the central metropolitan area sufficiently that it can substitute for rapid transit on some trips. That overlap is what you liked in Berlin and Paris. One specialist layer Here KL gives us several possibilities, but I would choose one, not all three: * monorail as a cheap, tight-turning inner-city elevated mode; * Sunway-style BRT as a medium-capacity orbital/feeder solution; * or airport express as a specialised fast limited-stop service. Of those, BRT is probably the most interesting gameplay mode because it can connect gaps between rail corridors rather than merely duplicate a downtown railway. The real Sunway BRT is already represented as a distinct mode in the integrated network. Why it could create genuinely different networks I can already see at least four credible architectures. 1. Kuala Lumpur-centred radial network Shah Alam ── PJ ── KL ── Ampang \ | / \ | / Puchong | Putrajaya Invest heavily in rapid transit through central Kuala Lumpur. KTM provides long outer radials. This produces a classic high-accessibility centre, but cross-suburban journeys tend to require central transfers. 2. Heavy regional backbone Build a strong KTM-style cross metropolitan network first. Then rapid transit becomes feeder and distributor: outer west ===== KL ===== north/east || || south Putrajaya The same passenger might either: * take rapid transit directly with many stops; or * travel to a regional station, use the fast trunk, then transfer back. That is proper substitution, not different-coloured lines. 3. Polycentric western network This may be the most interesting. Treat Petaling Jaya, Subang, Shah Alam and Puchong as important destinations rather than satellites of KL. Build lateral lines between them: Shah Alam │ Subang ── Petaling Jaya │ │ Sunway KL │ Puchong │ Putrajaya Now a large proportion of journeys never enter central Kuala Lumpur. Regional rail can handle one set of longer movements while rapid transit/BRT creates a western mesh. That is structurally different from Strategy 1. 4. Southern growth strategy Develop the Putrajaya/Cyberjaya and airport axis early. There is a particularly nice real-world precedent here: Putrajaya can be reached by the long MRT Putrajaya Line, while the airport corridor is also served by the much faster ERL layer. The MRT route runs from Kwasa Damansara through central and southern metropolitan areas to Putrajaya. So there can be a genuine choice between: fast sparse trunk versus slower dense metropolitan line to broadly the same part of the region. That is exactly the kind of modal overlap INTERCITY needs. Opening, middle and mature game The progression could be unusually good. Opening: Kuala Lumpur and Petaling Jaya form a fairly forgiving continuous urban area. The player gets considerable freedom to establish their own rapid-transit geometry rather than tracing corridors. Middle: Shah Alam, Subang, Puchong, Kajang and northern centres become significant. KTM starts becoming competitive with extending rapid transit indefinitely. Cross-suburban movements appear. Mature: Putrajaya/Cyberjaya and the longer metropolitan extremities matter. At this stage there may be several plausible route choices for the same journey: rapid transit → rapid transit versus rapid transit → regional → rapid transit versus BRT → regional and potentially a fast airport/southern corridor. That is substantially better than merely unlocking another section of a fixed corridor. Geography should influence, not prescribe I would keep: * major hills and ridgelines; * Klang/Gombak river structure; * the broad western and southern lowland development; * the long distance to Putrajaya and the airport; * multiple substantial suburban centres. But I would not make the rivers hard bridge constraints everywhere. They are not the Bosphorus. And I would not make the hills so restrictive that every rail line gets funnelled through a handful of gates. DBKL’s geography supports terrain being a cost/gradient influence rather than a Genoa-like absolute network skeleton. The biggest danger KL has too many named technologies. If we faithfully reproduce: MRT + LRT + KTM + monorail + BRT + ERL we could accidentally create six buttons that amount to perhaps three genuinely different transport behaviours. That is precisely the cosmetic-mode problem you identified. The transport history is useful inspiration, but the gameplay abstraction should be ruthless. Compared with your existing cities It does not feel like Bangkok to me after looking more closely. Bangkok’s identity is much more strongly a central metropolis divided and shaped by the Chao Phraya, with elevated systems radiating through it. KL would be: uneven terrain + continuous polycentric conurbation + overlapping urban/regional rail. Nor does it duplicate Greater Bay Area. GBA is fundamentally an intercity problem connecting major metropolitan systems. Klang Valley can remain one continuously interconnected network in which the distinction between “urban” and “regional” becomes fuzzy. That fuzziness is potentially its strongest feature. So I would keep KL More specifically, I’d shortlist it as: KLANG VALLEY One metropolis, several centres. Rapid transit fills the gaps; regional rail cuts across them. At this point I would put Barcelona, Stuttgart, Rotterdam–The Hague and Klang Valley in the serious group. KL has less spectacular geography than the first two, but possibly better freedom than either, because its geography shapes route economics without continually telling the player where the line must go.
